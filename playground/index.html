Council votes to bury the last mile of the riverfront freeway

By Lena Cho · City desk · 6:14 a.m.

A 28-acre lid park, seven years, and $1.4 billion — opponents called it a downtown gift wrapped as climate policy.

The river was never supposed to share a curb with six lanes of through-traffic, but for forty-one years it did. On Thursday night, Cedarport City Council voted 8–3 to bury the last remaining stretch of the Riverfront Expressway and build a lid park on top — a project staff say will take seven years and $1.4 billion.

Mayor Imani Brooks framed the vote as unfinished business from the 2019 climate plan. “We already moved the trucks. We already rebuilt the bridges. This is the part that actually gives the city back to people who walk,” she said, as a late-night gallery of cyclists and shopkeepers applauded from the rail.

Ward 4’s Tom Hale voted no and promised a referendum campaign. “You cannot lid your way out of a housing shortage,” he said. Construction staging begins Tuesday night with two westbound lanes closed through the core.

Transit

Night buses double in September. Weekend owls stay hourly.

By Sam Rivera · 7:02 a.m.

Weeknight frequency drops to 15 minutes on the 7, 12, and 40. Saturday after 1 a.m. is still a gamble.

Cedarport Transit will add 64 late trips on September 8, paid from the surplus the agency swore it did not have in March. Operators get a $2.10 night differential. The weekend owl — the one that actually gets kitchen staff home — stays hourly.

After 40 years, Marisol’s is leaving the market hall

By Priya Nandakumar · 8:11 a.m.

The cardamom bun survives. The steam-table lease does not.

Marisol Vega posted the notice on a paper bag: last market-hall Saturday is October 3. The new corner has a window wall, a proper hood, and rent that “only hurts on Tuesdays.” Regulars are already arguing about whether the buns will taste the same without the fish-stall weather.

Housing

Rents flatten for the first time since 2021

By Owen Park · 8:40 a.m.

Citywide vacancy ticked to 3.1%. One-bedrooms stopped climbing. That is not the same as cheap.

The quarterly rent survey landed like a weather report nobody quite trusts. Median asking rent for a one-bedroom held at $1,840. Purpose-built vacancies rose from 2.4% to 3.1%. Tenant organizers called it “a pause, not a thaw.” Landlords called it proof the new supply is working. Both cited the same spreadsheet.

The stadium went quiet, then loud, in the 89th

By Chris Adeyemi · 9:05 a.m.

New striker Kesi Lang scored her first Cedarport FC goal with a header nobody marked.

Lang arrived in July with a reputation for arriving late to the ball and earlier than everyone else to the near post. Last night she did both. The header was ugly and perfect. The north stand held the note for a full minute, which in this building is a liturgical event.

The civic library’s late rooms are booked out, so they are opening at dawn

By Hannah Ortiz · 9:28 a.m.

Saturday 6 a.m. reading rooms start next week. Bring a sweater. The radiators still think it is July.

Hold times for the quiet carrels hit eleven days in August, which is how a public building knows it is doing something right and also that it is too small. Starting September 5, the third-floor rooms open at 6 a.m. on Saturdays. Coffee is still banned near the atlases. The security desk asked us to mention that twice.

Science

Smoke season arrived two weeks early. The air map is the new weather.

By Dr. Elena Voss · 10:01 a.m.

AQI crossed 150 before lunch on Thursday. Clinics report the usual: kids, lungs, and people who “don’t usually have asthma.”

The ridge of high pressure that used to mean a beach day now means a closed window. Wildfire smoke from two valleys over settled on the city Thursday morning and did not leave. Public health posted the familiar diagram: N95 if you have one, cancel the 10K if you do not.

Opinion

We keep building housing like it is a specialty item

Editorial board · 10:20 a.m.

Cedarport does not have a mystery. It has a permitting calendar and a talent for calling that a conversation.

A city that can lid a freeway in seven years can approve a four-story walk-up in one. The fact that we treat the second as the harder project is not a law of physics. It is a habit. Habits can be voted out. Most of them are not.

Eastside robotics is going to Houston with a robot named after the lunch lady

By Maya Chen · 11:12 a.m.

“Doris 3” can intake a foam cube in 1.4 seconds. Doris the human still runs the better kitchen.

The Eastside Comp team qualified for the national invitational on a Thursday afternoon in a gym that smelled like floor wax and victory popcorn. Their robot, Doris 3, is named for cafeteria manager Doris Pell, who has packed the team’s late buses for six seasons. Pell said she is “honored and a little concerned about the liability.”
